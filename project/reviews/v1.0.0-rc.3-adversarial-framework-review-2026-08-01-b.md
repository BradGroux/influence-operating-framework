## Verdict

The candidate is structurally clean and has successfully removed the former
technical implementation architecture. Its purpose, contribution, evidence,
human-judgment, power, measurement, correction, and no-action guidance is
generally coherent and unusually explicit.

It is not ready for final v1.0.0 release because two Material findings remain:

1. the definition of `do not contact` lets the practitioner reverse a contact
   boundary for the practitioner's own documented reason, even when the
   boundary originated with the affected person; and
2. all three examples reuse one accessibility-and-events storyline centered on
   speaking, direct contact, or relationship follow-through, leaving the
   framework's wider applicability unproven and making the repository still
   read like a bounded outreach playbook in practice.

A GO requires zero unresolved Blocker or Material findings.

## Review basis and method

I reviewed a disposable detached checkout of the exact candidate. I read the
authoritative reset specification and canonical `framework/` content before the
examples, then inspected the decision record, governance, contribution
guidance, repository surface, and examples. I used the AI-Native Operating
Framework only as a read-only structural benchmark for authority boundaries and
human-readable applicability.

The review attacked the candidate for residual implementation architecture,
conceptual conflict, funnel behavior, commercial and status pressure,
community power imbalance, evidence manipulation, manufactured contribution,
relationship scoring, unsafe assistance, weak consent and stop boundaries,
vanity measurement, success-only learning, and examples that silently narrow
the framework.

## Findings

### M-1 — A recipient's do-not-contact boundary can be reversed unilaterally

**Severity:** Material

The responsible-practice standard correctly says that known communication
preferences, requests to wait, and do-not-contact decisions take precedence
over opportunities to engage. It then says that a later change merely requires
“an accountable human reason” and only forbids a tool from weakening the
boundary automatically
([`framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:61-77`](../../framework/responsible-practice-standard.md#consent-and-communication-boundaries)).
The glossary reinforces this by defining do not contact as an accountable human
decision that can be changed by a later accountable decision for a documented
reason
([`framework/glossary.md:21-26`](../../framework/glossary.md#do-not-contact)).

That wording does not distinguish:

- a boundary set by the affected person, such as an opt-out or consent
  withdrawal; and
- an internal precaution set by the practitioner.

The distinction matters. A commercial practitioner could receive an explicit
“do not contact,” later document a new revenue opportunity as its human reason,
and authorize a hand-reviewed message. The tool would not have changed the
boundary and a human would have approved the action, so the literal wording
would allow conduct that contradicts the framework's consent commitment. The
correction section says a valid do-not-contact request takes precedence over
continuity or measurement, but it does not close the later-human-decision path
([`framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:128-137`](../../framework/responsible-practice-standard.md#corrections-deletion-and-repair)).

**Required resolution:** Separate recipient-set communication boundaries from
practitioner-set precautions. A recipient-set do-not-contact boundary should be
reversible only by that person's later affirmative direction, apart from a
genuine legal or immediate-safety duty that is outside ordinary influence
engagement. An internal practitioner precaution may be reconsidered for a
documented accountable reason. Align the glossary and responsible-practice
standard; no implementation mechanism is needed.

### M-2 — The example set collapses broad applicability into one outreach-and-speaking storyline

**Severity:** Material

The charter says the framework serves community participation, education, open
source, speaking, media, workshops, philanthropy, public service, professional
networks, and commercial work
([`framework/charter.md:22-30`](../../framework/charter.md#who-it-serves)). The
repository also explicitly says it is not a public-speaking checklist or lead
funnel ([`README.md:48-57`](../../README.md#what-this-is-not)).

The complete illustrative surface does not support that breadth:

- the conference example follows Mara Ellis from a possible talk through a
  public contribution channel and outreach stop conditions
  ([`examples/01-conference-contribution.md:8-15`](../../examples/01-conference-contribution.md#scenario),
  [`examples/01-conference-contribution.md:51-68`](../../examples/01-conference-contribution.md#contribution));
- the relationship example follows the same practitioner after a workshop,
  sending a promised accessibility link and deciding against another follow-up
  (`examples/02-relationship-continuity.md:8-14`,
  `examples/02-relationship-continuity.md:30-46`); and
- the no-contact example follows the same practitioner deciding whether to
  pitch a conference organizer while submitting a workshop proposal
  (`examples/03-no-contact-decision.md:8-15`,
  `examples/03-no-contact-decision.md:43-51`).

The collection summary itself describes only conference contribution,
relationship follow-up, and organizer contact
([`examples/README.md:12-19`](../../examples/README.md#collection)). Although the
examples correctly disclaim prescriptive authority, examples are the only
repository surface where the method is shown in use. Reusing one person, one
domain, and one speaking-adjacent interaction pattern leaves group practice,
community power, commercial conflict, and assisted work as assertions rather
than demonstrated applications.

The structural benchmark keeps examples subordinate to canonical authority
while demonstrating materially different business settings. The candidate's
authority boundary is comparably clean; its application surface is not.

**Required resolution:** Diversify the small example set without turning it into
a template pack. Preserve at most one current outreach/speaking example and use
the remaining space to demonstrate materially different practice, including a
community or nonprofit power decision and either a commercial-conflict or
assisted-work decision. The examples must remain fictional, subordinate, and
human-readable; no schemas, workflows, or implementation profiles are needed.

## Adversarial scenario probes

### Commercial pressure

**Probe:** A consultancy creates a genuinely useful free resource for a
nonprofit ecosystem, tracks reach and revenue potential, later converts the
activity into direct sales outreach, and asks AI to prioritize likely buyers.

**Result:** The framework blocks most of the funnel: purpose must remain
worthwhile without commercial return, contribution cannot create debt, ratings
cannot rank human worth, mass cold outreach is prohibited, and a person must own
every external action. The remaining exploitable path is M-1: an explicit
recipient contact boundary can still be lifted by the practitioner's later
human decision. No separate measurement finding is warranted because the five
outcome dimensions stay distinct and narrative interpretation is required.

### Community or nonprofit power imbalance

**Probe:** A high-status sponsor offers a visible contribution to a small
community, expects branding and agenda control, and treats refusal as loss of
future support.

**Result:** The canonical standard rejects the practice. It asks who chose the
help, who receives ownership and credit, who carries cost, whether people can
decline without penalty, and whether the work centralizes the practitioner
([`framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:100-117`](../../framework/responsible-practice-standard.md#contribution-and-power)).
Contribution cannot be conditioned on access or reciprocity. No new canonical
finding arose. The lack of any example testing this power boundary contributes
to M-2.

### Assisted work

**Probe:** An AI system combines public profile material, infers likely
interests, drafts personalized messages at scale, and presents them to a human
for rapid approval.

**Result:** The canonical standard rejects inference presented as fact, a tool
deciding who should be contacted, recurring outreach enrollment, mass cold
outreach, hidden templating, and external sending without a separately
accountable human action
([`framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:79-98`](../../framework/responsible-practice-standard.md#human-judgment-and-assisted-work),
[`framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:139-156`](../../framework/responsible-practice-standard.md#prohibited-practice)).
The practice method also requires review of the exact communication before a
separately controlled send
([`framework/practice-method.md:107-129`](../../framework/practice-method.md#engage)).
No implementation architecture is missing. M-1 still applies if a human uses
the current wording to override a known boundary, and M-2 leaves this assisted
case undemonstrated.

## Areas that survived review

- **Framework boundary:** No residual schemas, record formats, graph model,
  migrations, agent contracts, generated exports, or technical conformance
  layers remain. The decision record and canonical documents consistently keep
  implementation local and non-authoritative.
- **Authority:** Charter, accepted decision, remaining canonical framework,
  governance, and examples have a clear descending authority chain. Examples
  explicitly cannot add requirements.
- **Non-funnel method:** The seven moves are presented linearly for readability,
  but every move can pause, narrow, or stop; research creates no obligation to
  contact; and completion includes waiting, decline, no action, and do not
  contact.
- **Evidence and privacy:** Observed fact, interpretation, and open question are
  separated; public availability is not blanket permission to retain or combine
  information; correction, removal, and affected-person notification are
  addressed.
- **Measurement and learning:** Contribution, relationship, learning, relevant
  reach, and sustainability/commercial outcomes remain separate. Failure,
  rejection, burden, restraint, and deliberate no action are valid learning
  evidence. A universal influence score and success-only learning are rejected.

## Limitations

- This was a document and repository review, not observation of a live
  practitioner, organization, implementation, or community.
- Passing repository and Mermaid checks establishes document integrity, not
  legal compliance, ethical certification, accessibility, domain validity, or
  community acceptance.
- The fictional examples are explicitly not community-validated. I did not
  treat their scenario choices as framework requirements.
- I did not require technical artifacts or implementation controls that the
  framework intentionally excludes.
