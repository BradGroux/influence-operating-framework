## Executive summary

The candidate successfully removes the former technical implementation
architecture and provides coherent guidance on purpose, contribution, evidence,
human judgment, power, measurement, correction, and no-action outcomes.

Two Material findings prevent release: a recipient-set do-not-contact boundary
can be reversed by the practitioner, and all three examples remain concentrated
in one speaking-and-outreach storyline.

## Findings

### Blocker

None.

### Material

#### M-01: Recipient-set do-not-contact boundary can be reversed unilaterally

**Evidence:** `framework/glossary.md:21-26` at the reviewed commit.

The glossary permits an accountable practitioner to reconsider a do-not-contact
decision for a documented reason without distinguishing who set the boundary.
That allows a practitioner to override a boundary communicated by the affected
person.

Required resolution: distinguish recipient-set boundaries from internal
practitioner precautions. Only the affected person or their authorized
representative may affirmatively change a recipient-set boundary.

#### M-02: Example set does not demonstrate the framework's claimed breadth

**Evidence:** `README.md:7-11` and `examples/README.md:14-19` at the reviewed
commit.

All three examples reuse one accessibility-and-events storyline centered on
speaking, direct contact, or follow-through. The framework claims broader use
across contribution, communities, sponsorship, public service, education, and
other contexts, but the examples make it read like an outreach playbook.

Required resolution: retain a small example set while demonstrating materially
different contexts, including a group power decision and useful work that does
not depend on direct outreach.
