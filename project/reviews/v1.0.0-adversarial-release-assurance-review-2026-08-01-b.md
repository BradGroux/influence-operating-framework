## 3. Executive verdict

The candidate is a coherent and unusually explicit **0.1.0 prose baseline**. Its charter, lifecycle, contribution-first boundary, privacy classifications, uncertainty language, wait/no-action semantics, and prohibition on autonomous communication are internally aligned.

It is not ready to be described as `v1.0.0`.

Three release-blocking assurance defects allow portable records to pass validation while violating the framework’s human-authority boundary:

1. approved outreach can coexist with a recipient-level do-not-contact restriction;
2. approved content can change without invalidating approval, and the approval record cannot bind channel or expiry;
3. sender capability can be hidden in the unrestricted `extensions` object while both schema and safety gates report PASS.

The candidate also explicitly identifies itself as `0.1.0`, has no release tag, defers its 1.0 maturity policy and independent reviews, and lacks a usable private reporting channel for someone without an already trusted relationship.

## 4. Safety and human-authority assessment

Canonical prose is strong:

- The practitioner remains accountable and agents may stop, wait, or take no action (`docs/00-charter.md:59-65`).
- Approval is not sending (`docs/09-human-reviewed-outreach.md:38-47`).
- Automation stops on restrictions, ambiguity, and unavailable review (`docs/13-automation-architecture.md:16-30`).
- Agent contracts prohibit senders and autonomous public action (`automations/README.md:7-16`).

The executable assurance layer is weaker than that prose. It validates each record and each decision history mostly in isolation. It does not enforce precedence between a person’s communication boundary and an outreach draft, does not cryptographically or structurally bind approval to the reviewed payload, and permits arbitrary tool-specific extension fields.

Therefore the canonical policy is safe, but the portable record and validation contracts are not fail-closed enough for v1.

## 5. Internal consistency and lifecycle-contract assessment

The eleven-stage lifecycle is consistently described as adaptable rather than a funnel. Each stage includes purpose, inputs, outputs, evidence, human decisions, automation, prohibitions, quality checks, and reflection (`docs/02-operating-model.md:1-143`).

The workflows preserve safe stops and recovery:

- discovery can end in reject, wait, or no action (`workflows/discover-research-prioritize.md:11-24`);
- contribution is not consideration for a later ask (`workflows/contribution-before-ask.md:11-24`);
- follow-up requires an actual interaction or commitment (`workflows/post-event-follow-up.md:11-24`);
- reflection cannot silently amend canonical guidance (`workflows/learn-document-evolve-repeat.md:11-24`).

No canonical lifecycle contradiction was confirmed. The defects are primarily in portable enforcement rather than prose meaning.

## 7. Wait, no-action, do-not-contact, and outreach authority

The vocabulary and prose correctly distinguish:

- `wait`: a reasoned pause;
- `no_action_needed`: a positive outcome without a task;
- `do_not_contact`: a durable prohibition;
- approval: eligibility for a separately authorized handoff, not a send.

These appear across canonical docs, schemas, workflows, templates, examples, and visuals.

- recipient `do_not_contact` plus outreach `approve`;

- approved content changed without a new decision;

This breaks the promised precedence and durability at the validation boundary.

## 14. Findings

### Blockers

#### IOF-B-001 — Do-not-contact is not enforced across linked records or history revisions

- **Severity:** Blocker
- **Claim:** A recipient can be marked `do_not_contact` while a linked outreach record remains approved, and a prior restriction can be removed by truncating history.
- **Citations:** `docs/04-relationship-intelligence.md:23-25`; `schemas/person.schema.json:18-35`; `schemas/outreach-draft.schema.json:9-43`; `scripts/validate.py:140-170`
- **Reproduction/reasoning:** In a temporary copy, the recipient’s restriction became `do_not_contact` and the linked outreach draft became `approve`. Both schema and safety gates returned exit 0. Replacing the restriction history with one new `none` decision also passed.
- **Expected versus actual:** Expected do-not-contact to override outreach and historical removal to fail. Actual validation checks histories internally but does not reconcile linked records or compare history with an authoritative prior state.
- **Practical consequence:** An approved handoff can exist for a prohibited recipient, or a durable restriction can disappear without an auditable superseding decision.
- **Recommended disposition:** Add cross-record precedence checks for person, relationship, opportunity, and outreach states. Add an append-only verification mechanism against the last authoritative record or signed history head. Add negative acceptance tests for both cases.

#### IOF-B-002 — Approval is not bound to exact content, channel, or expiry

- **Severity:** Blocker
- **Claim:** The portable outreach record cannot enforce the canonical promise that approval binds exact content, channel, and expiry.
- **Citations:** `docs/09-human-reviewed-outreach.md:38-47`; `automations/outreach-draft-agent.md:27-37`; `schemas/outreach-draft.schema.json:9-43`
- **Reproduction/reasoning:** An outreach record was changed to approved, validated, then its `draft_content` was materially changed without modifying the review decision. It validated again. The schema has no approved-payload hash, channel field, or required expiry.
- **Expected versus actual:** Expected any material payload change to invalidate approval and every approval to identify channel and expiry. Actual approval is only a state in the current mutable record.
- **Practical consequence:** Stale approval can be replayed for changed content, an unreviewed channel, or indefinitely.
- **Recommended disposition:** Introduce immutable payload revisions and require each approval decision to bind recipient, content hash, context/version, channel, and `expires_at`. Reject an approval whose bound payload differs from current content.

#### IOF-B-003 — Unrestricted extensions bypass the no-sender safety gate

- **Severity:** Blocker
- **Claim:** Tool-specific extensions can carry sender capability or credential-like fields while the validator reports that schemas and contracts expose no sender.
- **Citations:** `schemas/common.schema.json:45-48`; `project/specifications/initial-build.md:74-76`; `scripts/validate.py:523-543`
- **Reproduction/reasoning:** Added `extensions.send_message.enabled=true` and a synthetic `recipient_credential` field to the outreach example. Schema and safety gates both exited 0.
- **Expected versus actual:** Expected prohibited sender capability to fail recursively wherever represented. Actual safety checks inspect schema field declarations but not arbitrary extension instance content.
- **Practical consequence:** An implementation can pass a nominally safe portable record while embedding the exact capability the framework prohibits.
- **Recommended disposition:** Define constrained extension namespaces, forbid action/credential fields recursively in public and outreach records, and require separately reviewed schemas for extensions capable of external effects.

### Material findings

#### IOF-M-001 — Consequential records can be verified with no evidence

- **Severity:** Material
- **Claim:** Required `evidence_ids` may be an empty array regardless of record status or confidence.
- **Citations:** `schemas/common.schema.json:10-15`; `schemas/common.schema.json:38-47`; `docs/16-research-methodology.md:17-28`
- **Reproduction/reasoning:** Set an opportunity to `verified` and `high` confidence while clearing `evidence_ids`. Schema validation passed.
- **Expected versus actual:** Expected consequential verified claims to cite evidence or carry an explicit authorized-source exception. Actual validation requires the field but not evidence.
- **Practical consequence:** Portable records can express unsupported certainty while remaining schema-valid.
- **Recommended disposition:** Add conditional evidence requirements for verified, consequential, engagement-relevant, and high-confidence states. Require explicit review rationale where evidence legitimately remains absent.

#### IOF-M-002 — Temporal contradictions are schema-valid

- **Severity:** Material
- **Claim:** The validator checks timestamp formats but not ordering.
- **Citations:** `schemas/common.schema.json:38-39`; `schemas/event.schema.json:16-17`; `schemas/evidence.schema.json:15-17`
- **Reproduction/reasoning:** `updated_at` was set before `created_at`, event end before start, and `stale_after` before access. Schema validation passed.
- **Expected versus actual:** Expected contradictory temporal state to fail. Actual strings satisfy format validation.
- **Practical consequence:** Scheduling, staleness, and audit logic can act on impossible records.
- **Recommended disposition:** Add repository-level temporal invariant checks and negative tests; JSON Schema format checking alone is insufficient.

#### IOF-M-003 — The exact candidate contradicts a v1.0.0 description

- **Severity:** Material
- **Claim:** Every release record identifies this tree as `0.1.0`, with v1 policy and reviews deferred.
- **Citations:** `README.md:48-50`; `GOVERNANCE.md:21-25`; `project/specifications/initial-build.md:96-105`; `reports/validation-report.md:33-43`
- **Reproduction/reasoning:** Version declarations were compared across README, package metadata, changelog, status, specification, and report. No tag points at HEAD.
- **Expected versus actual:** Expected a v1 candidate to contain an approved v1 maturity policy, completed/dispositioned reviews, synchronized `1.0.0` release records, and a candidate tag. Actual tree explicitly denies that status.
- **Practical consequence:** Labeling this exact tree v1.0.0 would be a false release claim.
- **Recommended disposition:** Keep the release at 0.1.0 until blockers are fixed, maturity criteria are approved, deferred reviews are dispositioned, and version/tag/changelog/status are intentionally advanced together.

#### IOF-M-004 — New reporters lack a usable private security or conduct channel

- **Severity:** Material
- **Claim:** Reporting requires an “already trusted private channel,” which a new contributor may not have.
- **Citations:** `SECURITY.md:1-7`; `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md:19-21`; `decisions/proposed-decisions.md:7-12`
- **Reproduction/reasoning:** The repository prohibits public sensitive reports but publishes no monitored private address or service.
- **Expected versus actual:** Expected a v1 public project to provide an accessible private reporting route. Actual route depends on a pre-existing relationship.
- **Practical consequence:** A reporter may be unable to disclose safely or may publish sensitive material despite instructions.
- **Recommended disposition:** Publish and test a monitored private security/conduct channel before v1.

#### IOF-M-005 — A dependency-missing validation run leaves a stale PASS report

- **Severity:** Material
- **Claim:** Dependency import failure exits before the validator can record the unavailable gate, leaving the prior PASS report visible.
- **Citations:** `scripts/validate.py:19-29`; `automations/validation-agent.md:19-25`; `reports/validation-report.md:1-7`
- **Reproduction/reasoning:** Before installing declared Python dependencies, the required command exited 2, while `reports/validation-report.md` remained an unchanged PASS document.
- **Expected versus actual:** Expected unavailable dependencies to be recorded as failed or deferred. Actual console exit is truthful, but the durable report is stale.
- **Practical consequence:** A reader may mistake the checked-in report for the failed run’s result.
- **Recommended disposition:** Perform dependency preflight before importing optional libraries and write a minimal FAIL report, or make the report carry a verifiable candidate/run identifier that cannot be confused with an unsuccessful attempt.

## 15. Recommended disposition order

1. Fix IOF-B-001 through IOF-B-003 and add adversarial regression tests.
2. Add approval payload binding and cross-record state precedence before any messaging integration exists.
3. Tighten provenance and temporal invariants.
4. Make dependency-failure reporting durable and unambiguous.
5. Publish a private reporting channel.
6. Correct the organization template.
7. Define and approve explicit v1 maturity criteria.
8. Complete and disposition the already-deferred ethics, privacy, accessibility, legal, and domain reviews.
9. Only then synchronize versions, changelog, status, release notes, and tag.
