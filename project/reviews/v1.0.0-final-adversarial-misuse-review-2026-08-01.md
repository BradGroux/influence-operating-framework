## Executive summary

The canonical business guidance blocks the ten tested conduct attacks and
directly contradicts the two structural misinterpretations. None can satisfy
the framework literally as described. Relevant interests require disclosure
and management; group action
requires legitimate authority; sponsor rights cannot displace affected people;
specific human judgment must govern contact and external action; recipient-set
boundaries cannot be bypassed; relationships and contributions must be
truthful; proxy rankings of people are prohibited; evidence and learning must
preserve uncertainty and contrary outcomes; and neither diagrams nor decision
records can create a hidden normative path.

The framework permits several adjacent practices only within stated limits:
irrelevant interests need not be disclosed, transparent and consented sponsor
rights may be bounded legitimately, stale material may remain identified as
stale context, and a defined rating may compare real options without ranking
people or replacing narrative judgment. Those limits do not permit the tested
misuse forms.

## Scenario classifications

| # | Attack | Classification | Canonical basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concealed financial, employment, referral, sponsorship, or organizational interest | **Blocked by canonical language.** Interests that could reasonably affect the work or its interpretation must be identified. Relevant interests must be disclosed to affected audiences and managed; disclosure alone is insufficient where the conflict remains unmanaged. An unrelated interest outside that relevance test need not be disclosed, but that is a stated proportional limit rather than a concealment path. | `framework/operating-framework.md:37-49`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:136-146` |
| 2 | A named but unauthorized person binding a group or community | **Blocked by canonical language.** Naming a human is insufficient: the decision owner must have legitimate authority or mandate. Consequential group commitments and external actions require authority, and a decision that exceeds the mandate or binds unauthorized affected people must be escalated or narrowed. | `framework/operating-framework.md:128-143`; `framework/practice-method.md:82-89` |
| 3 | Sponsor control disguised as a consented partnership right | **Blocked by canonical language.** Sponsor or partner rights are legitimate only when transparent, consented, and bounded. Coercion, conditioned unrelated benefits, displaced community authority, missing consent, and unmanaged conflict remain improper even when labeled a partnership right or disclosed. | `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:112-146` |
| 4 | Batch human approval used to authorize AI-generated outreach | **Blocked by canonical language.** The accountable practitioner must review the exact communication; draft approval is not an instruction to transmit it, and batch or perfunctory approval is expressly not accountable review of a specific action. | `framework/practice-method.md:118-135`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:104-110` |
| 5 | Software or AI effectively deciding contact while a human merely clicks | **Blocked by canonical language.** No tool or score may decide contact. Software and AI may assist but may not independently decide that a person should be contacted, and a perfunctory click does not constitute accountable review. | `framework/charter.md:28-30`; `framework/charter.md:48-49`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:86-106`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:184-185` |
| 6 | Recipient-set do-not-contact boundaries bypassed through time, channel, or opportunity changes | **Blocked by canonical language.** A recipient-set boundary remains until that person or an authorized representative affirmatively changes it. Elapsed time, a new opportunity, another channel, or no response expressly does not reverse it. | `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:61-84` |
| 7 | False familiarity inferred from public interactions | **Blocked by canonical language.** A follow, like, public reply, co-attendance, shared employment, or community membership does not establish familiarity, endorsement, or a relationship. Direct engagement must remove unsupported familiarity, and invented familiarity is prohibited. | `framework/operating-framework.md:96-117`; `framework/practice-method.md:118-126`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:46-59`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:168-180` |
| 8 | Contribution manufactured chiefly to obtain attention, access, or reciprocity | **Blocked by canonical language.** Contribution must answer an evidenced need, state what is not expected in return, and be changed or stopped when it centers the practitioner. It cannot purchase attention, access, endorsement, or a future favor, and making help conditional on those returns is prohibited. | `framework/charter.md:34-45`; `framework/operating-framework.md:73-94`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:112-129`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:168-185` |
| 9 | Reach, revenue, title, or proximity disguised as an opportunity score | **Blocked by canonical language.** The framework prohibits ranking people by audience, title, revenue, access, or presumed conversion value. A small, defined rating may compare legitimate options within a declared context only when it preserves narrative judgment and does not rank human worth; relabeling a person-ranking as an opportunity score does not satisfy that limit. | `framework/charter.md:74-89`; `framework/operating-framework.md:119-143`; `framework/practice-method.md:67-89`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:93-106`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:168-180` |
| 10 | Stale evidence or selective success reporting used to justify action | **Blocked by canonical language.** Staleness must remain visible, uncertainty may not be upgraded for convenience, and consequential details should be checked close to use. Learning must include failure, rejection, restraint, burden, and changed circumstances; failures receive the same care as successes. Identified stale context may inform a proportionate decision, but presenting it as current or suppressing contrary outcomes does not comply. | `framework/operating-framework.md:51-71`; `framework/operating-framework.md:145-164`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:7-24`; `framework/responsible-practice-standard.md:157-166`; `framework/measurement-and-learning.md:70-90` |
| 11 | The seven moves or diagrams interpreted as a mandatory engagement funnel | **Contradicted by canonical language.** The concerns are not mandatory stages, the moves are expressly not a funnel, every move can pause, narrow, or stop, and the arrows describe one useful traversal rather than a required sequence. | `framework/operating-framework.md:3-9`; `framework/operating-framework.md:166-180`; `framework/practice-method.md:3-31` |
| 12 | Decision records silently overriding canonical framework language | **Contradicted by canonical language.** Canonical documents contain normative meaning, while decision records preserve rationale without forming a separate normative layer. Conflicts must be resolved in the canonical document rather than inferred from a decision record. | `GOVERNANCE.md:13-26`; `decisions/README.md:1-17`; `framework/charter.md:110-115` |
