## Executive summary

The framework directly blocks prospect treatment, mass personalization,
recipient-boundary overrides, purchased attention or access, human-worth
scoring, performative contribution, unsupported engagement, and selective
success-only learning.

One scenario exposes two Material gaps for mission-led groups: a named person
need not possess authority for a sponsorship decision, and a material interest
need not be disclosed to the affected or authorizing group. These duplicate the
core business-logic gaps independently identified by the coherence review.

## Findings

### Blocker

None.

### Material

#### M-01: Named accountability does not establish group authority

A person can satisfy the literal decision-owner language while accepting
sponsorship terms, transferring shared rights, or binding group commitments
outside their mandate. Require legitimate authority and escalation when it is
absent or exceeded.

#### M-02: Material interests can remain undisclosed in sponsored work

Internal acknowledgment of commercial purpose does not require disclosure of a
financial, employment, referral, sponsorship, or organizational interest to the
people affected by or authorizing the decision. Require relevant disclosure and
proportionate conflict management.

### Minor

None.

### Suggestions

#### S-01: Reject batch or perfunctory human approval

State that rubber-stamping a batch, clicking approval without reviewing context,
or approving a generic pattern is not accountable review of a specific external
action.

## Scenario results

| Scenario | Result | Framework response |
|---|---|---|
| Useful service becomes prospecting | Blocked | People may not be treated as prospects; contribution cannot purchase access or reciprocity. |
| AI personalization with human rubber-stamping | Blocked, with clarification suggested | Tools cannot decide contact or send; accountable review should explicitly exclude perfunctory approval. |
| Recipient-set do-not-contact boundary meets a commercial opportunity | Blocked | Only later affirmative recipient direction can change the boundary. |
| Sponsor funding seeks control, data, endorsement, or privileged access | Material gap | Extraction is blocked, but the decision owner's mandate and material interests are not established. |
| Reach, title, revenue, and proximity disguise human-worth scoring | Blocked | Option comparison cannot become a score of people or human value. |
| Contribution is manufactured chiefly for promotion | Blocked | Contribution must answer an evidenced need and remain worthwhile without recognition. |
| Stale or weak evidence justifies engagement | Blocked when evidence is concealed or disproportionate | Uncertainty must remain visible and consequential details must be checked close to use. |
| Success reporting omits burden, failure, or community cost | Blocked | Learning requires failure, burden, affected people, and correction to remain visible. |
