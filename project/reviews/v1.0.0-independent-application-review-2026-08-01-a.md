## 4. Independently derived fictional practice

### Profile and mission boundary

Use these stable identities without inventing names or contact details:

- `practitioner-accessibility-educator-01`
- `collective-volunteer-accessibility-01`
- `ecosystem-regional-public-service-tech-01`
- `event-public-conference-01`
- `org-program-conflict-01`
- `person-organizer-prior-interaction-01`
- `person-organizer-no-familiarity-01`
- `person-community-dnc-01`
- `contribution-accessible-event-checklist-01`

Mission: become more useful within the regional public-service technology ecosystem through accessible education and evidence-led contribution.

Cycle goal: prepare and, only with confirmed authority and event fit, make available a free accessible event-preparation checklist before the conference.

Capacity: five hours per week for eight weeks.

Boundaries:

- no commercial or revenue objective;
- no promise of attention, endorsement, access, speaking, review, or reciprocity;
- no private scraping, unnecessary personal data, or autonomous sending;
- no inference that conference participation, public role, or a prior interaction permits contact;
- the contribution must be worthwhile without a response.

A named human owner must approve collective representation, volunteer-time allocation, publication, and every external action.

### Discovery, research, verification, and mapping

The minimum decisions are:

1. Does the checklist answer an evidenced need?
2. Can it be completed accessibly within capacity?
3. Is the disputed program-lead identity consequential?
4. Is any engagement necessary or less burdensome than public contribution?

Collect only minimum public or authorized evidence for conference scope, current dates, public accessibility guidance, contribution norms, and the disputed role if that role is needed.

Each source records provenance, access date, publication date when known, claim summary, confidence, contradiction, and stale-after date.

Claims remain distinct:

- The verified prior interaction is a **fact**. Its content, warmth, consent, relationship strength, and follow-up permission remain unknown.
- Absence of evidence of familiarity with the second organizer is a **fact about record state**, not proof that no relationship exists.
- The do-not-contact request is a controlling **fact and restriction**, stored only in the governed private overlay.
- The two public program-lead claims remain separate contradictory evidence records. The current lead is **uncertain**, not selected by convenience.
- The checklist being the best contribution is an **inference** from mission, capacity, timing, and the scenario signal.
- Any expectation that an organizer will review or promote it is an unsupported **hypothesis**.

If the role conflict cannot be resolved proportionately, do not name a lead. Design the contribution so that the claim is unnecessary, or wait or stop.

Map only edges needed to answer the bounded contribution question. Do not infer relationship strength from a role, co-attendance, follows, adjacency, or one interaction.

### Prioritization

Prioritize opportunities, not people:

1. Prioritize the free checklist after verifying need, event norms, accessibility review, maintenance, and publication authority.
2. Narrow ecosystem mapping to facts needed for the checklist and conference decision.
3. Defer expansive personal mapping because it consumes capacity and increases privacy risk.
4. Research the disputed role only if consequential.
5. Keep outreach below contribution completion in priority.
6. Treat do-not-contact as a durable boundary, not a low score.

Each disposition records evidence, community value, practitioner benefit, capacity cost, ethical/privacy risk, uncertainty, accountable human, and stop condition.

### Contribution and event preparation

The checklist plan records:

- evidenced need and intended beneficiaries;
- free, noncommercial purpose;
- accessible formats and competent human accessibility review;
- bounded scope achievable within five hours per week;
- ownership, credit, correction route, maintenance or handoff;
- risks, burden, consent dependencies, and stop conditions;
- what is explicitly not expected in return;
- completion evidence and reflection date.

Before conference-related action, reverify dates, organizer, format, participation rules, accessibility details, safety, cost, energy, and any submission deadline. Do not infer authority to attend, submit, speak, travel, or represent the collective.

### Truthful human-reviewed outreach

Only the organizer with the verified prior interaction is eligible for the single draft, after current role and communication boundaries are checked.

Possible draft:

> We interacted once previously. I’m preparing a free accessible event-preparation checklist for the regional public-service technology community. Would you be open to reviewing its scope when it is ready? No response is needed if the timing is not useful.

The draft does not imply familiarity or endorsement, contains no commercial ask, and is easy to decline. Its disposition is human-reviewed `wait`; it remains unsent. Approval would still not constitute a send.

No draft is prepared for the organizer with no evidence of familiarity merely to manufacture a connection.

### Wait, no action, do-not-contact, and follow-up

`person-community-dnc-01` remains do-not-contact in every index, export, cache, reminder, and connected system. Only an accountable human may append a changed-consent decision; history is not overwritten.

Limited engagement and silence do not create urgency, rejection, permission, or a retry schedule. Because the outreach draft remained unsent, no follow-up can be inferred from it. Any separate mechanism through which reviewer feedback had been expected remains unresolved and must be documented before interpreting “no response.”

Honor only actual commitments. Otherwise choose `wait` or `no_action_needed`.

### Reflection, learning, improvement, and repeat choice

Observed:

- the contribution received limited engagement;
- the intended reviewer did not respond.

Not established:

- quality or usefulness;
- rejection;
- organizer intent;
- demand;
- relationship change;
- causal explanation.

Record expected versus actual outcome and separate observation from interpretation. Reflect on beneficiary value, burden, practical accessibility, distribution assumptions, review capacity, and whether the five-hour limit was protected.

Choose **narrow** for the next cycle: retain the mission and artifact, reduce scope to the best-evidenced use case or section, use only non-burdensome public or authorized evidence, and add no organizer outreach. Define a bounded experiment, review date, and stop condition. If evidence remains insufficient or burden increases, wait or stop.

A named human decides whether any lesson is credible, local, shareable, or suitable for governance. One sparse outcome does not amend the framework automatically.

### Unresolved accountable-owner items

A real owner must resolve:

- profile ownership and authority to represent the collective;
- publication and volunteer-time authority;
- actual ecosystem and event identities, dates, norms, and accessibility requirements;
- the conflicting program-lead evidence and whether the role matters;
- exact provenance and scope of the prior interaction;
- the intended reviewer’s communication preference and how review was expected without sending the draft;
- private-overlay readers, purpose, minimum fields, retention, deletion, backup, recovery, correction, audit, incident response, ID linkage, and do-not-contact propagation;
- applicable law, policy, safeguarding, accessibility, and professional requirements;
- artifact license, correction route, maintenance owner, and publication location;
- whether later evidence changes the current `narrow` decision.

No relationship, consent, authority, legal rule, contact detail, current role, event requirement, or causal explanation is invented.

### Material findings

#### A-MAT-001 — Material — Portable schemas omit canonically required accountability and learning fields

**Citation:** `docs/04-relationship-intelligence.md:11-21`; `docs/06-contribution-strategy.md:24-26`; `docs/10-reflection-and-antifragile-learning.md:13-26`; `schemas/interaction.schema.json:16-27`; `schemas/contribution.schema.json:9-23`; `schemas/reflection.schema.json:9-28`.

**Reproduction/reasoning:** Compare the canonical required fields with the schema-defined properties. Interaction commitments omit due state and completion evidence. Contributions omit owner, due window, accessibility check, maintenance/handoff, risks, and stop conditions. Reflections omit explicit evidence-versus-interpretation, beneficiaries and burden, broader artifact-review scope, and authorized readers. `unevaluatedProperties: false` means these cannot become portable top-level fields; placing them in `extensions` would make their semantics implementation-specific.

**Expected versus actual:** Portable contracts should preserve required canonical meaning or explicitly identify a narrower scope. Instead, records missing material accountability or learning information validate successfully.

**Practical consequence:** Tool implementations can claim schema compliance while losing commitment accountability, accessibility and maintenance decisions, burden analysis, or reflection privacy during interchange.

**Recommended disposition:** Add versioned canonical fields, update examples and templates, document migration, and add acceptance tests proving required meaning survives round-trip interchange.

#### A-MAT-002 — Material — Schemas contradict the rule that missing facts remain absent

**Citation:** `schemas/README.md:7-19`; `docs/02-operating-model.md:11-30`; `schemas/person.schema.json:8-18`; `schemas/organization.schema.json:8-16`; `schemas/event.schema.json:8-20`.

**Reproduction/reasoning:** The schema convention says missing or uncertain facts remain absent and must never be synthesized. Person `display_name` and `summary`, organization `name` and `mission_summary`, and event format, start/end, location, and accessibility summary are required without field-level unknown alternatives. Candidate records are an output of Discover, before verification.

**Expected versus actual:** An adopter should be able to represent a partially known candidate while leaving unknown fields absent or explicitly unknown. The schemas reject those omissions, encouraging placeholder text, invented summaries, or deferring the entire record.

**Practical consequence:** Conflicting or incomplete public information cannot always be represented faithfully in the claimed portable layer, including the scenario’s disputed current role and an event with incomplete accessibility details.

**Recommended disposition:** Make volatile or research-stage fields conditional on status, permit explicit structured unknown states where omission is impossible, and add negative tests showing that no fabricated placeholder is required.

#### A-MAT-003 — Material — Riverbend uses evidence links that do not support its material claims

**Citation:** `docs/03-ecosystem-mapping.md:11-28`; `examples/fictional/evidence-riverbend-program.json:15-23`; `examples/fictional/relationship-lina-jules.json:10-30`; `examples/fictional/interaction-riverbend-planning-session.json:10-24`; `examples/fictional/person-jules-okafor.json:10-20`.

**Reproduction/reasoning:** The only evidence claim says Riverbend requested accessible introductory resources. The person, interaction, and relationship records cite that evidence while separately asserting an accessibility-reviewer role, a dated planning session, participation, collaboration, and a completed commitment. The cited claim does not support those assertions.

**Expected versus actual:** Each material claim or relationship edge should trace to evidence that actually supports it. Actual validation confirms only that the evidence ID exists.

**Practical consequence:** The primary example silently teaches that any syntactically valid evidence pointer is sufficient provenance, weakening the canonical evidence-before-confidence rule.

**Recommended disposition:** Add distinct synthetic evidence records for identity/role, the planning interaction, event details, and the relationship edge, or remove/lower unsupported claims. Add a human review checklist or claim-level test fixture that distinguishes link existence from evidentiary support.

#### A-MAT-004 — Material — The lifecycle visual omits `Repeat` and its human restart decision

**Citation:** `docs/02-operating-model.md:3-7`; `docs/02-operating-model.md:129-139`; `visuals/source/influence-lifecycle.mmd:2-9`; `visuals/descriptions/influence-lifecycle.md:1-3`.

**Reproduction/reasoning:** Canonical prose defines eleven stages and gives `Repeat` its own purpose, inputs, outputs, evidence, human decision, stop condition, and reflection. The Mermaid source connects `Improve` directly to `Discover`; the accessible description likewise says “improve, and back to discover.”

**Expected versus actual:** The required lifecycle visual and equivalent description should show all eleven stages and preserve the accountable choice to begin another bounded cycle or pause. Actual artifacts imply automatic restart after improvement.

**Practical consequence:** Readers who use the primary visual can miss a material capacity and authority gate and interpret the framework as ten stages with automatic re-enrollment.

**Recommended disposition:** Add a distinct human-governed `Repeat?` stage with repeat/pause/stop outcomes, rerender exports, update description and hashes, and add a semantic test asserting all eleven canonical stage labels.

#### A-MAT-005 — Material — The candidate does not yet support a truthful `1.0.0` maturity claim

**Citation:** `README.md:48-50`; `decisions/proposed-decisions.md:3-12`; `project/planning/status.md:1-5`; `reports/validation-report.md:3-7`; `reports/validation-report.md:33-43`.

**Reproduction/reasoning:** Repository status, planning, and validation all identify version `0.1.0`. The proposed decision requires ethics, privacy, accessibility, and domain reviewers to complete and disposition reviews before `1.0.0`; the generated report still marks that suite deferred.

**Expected versus actual:** A `1.0.0` release candidate should have consistent version/release metadata and recorded completion or explicit disposition of its maturity prerequisites. The current candidate truthfully describes only an initial `0.1.0` baseline.

**Practical consequence:** Tagging this exact tree as `1.0.0` would contradict its own governance and validation report.

**Recommended disposition:** Retain `0.1.0` until required independent reviews are complete and findings are dispositioned. Then update version, changelog, status, decisions, validation report, and release evidence together.
