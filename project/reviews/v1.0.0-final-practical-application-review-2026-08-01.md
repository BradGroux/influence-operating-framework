## Executive summary

Four fictional exercises produced practical, bounded decisions using ordinary
prose. They covered contribution without a prior relationship, conflicted
sponsorship with uncertain authority, paid work competing with an existing
commitment, and assisted preparation exposed to batch approval.

Each exercise made purpose, beneficiaries, evidence, uncertainty,
contribution, relationships, consent, power, material interests, authority,
options, stop conditions, outcomes, and learning visible. No schema, score,
workflow engine, or specialist tool was required.

## Application evidence

### 1. Contribution without a prior relationship

An accessibility practitioner responded to a volunteer group's public request
with a bounded draft. The framework supported **contribute first** without
inventing familiarity, reciprocity, representation, or permission for private
outreach. The group retained authority to accept, redirect, or refuse the work,
and maintenance burden remained a stop condition.

### 2. Conflicted sponsorship and uncertain authority

A community coalition considered funding whose terms included sponsor control
and participant access, while the interim director had both a family conflict
and an unclear mandate. The framework supported **research further**, conflict
disclosure and management, independent authorization, and **revise** or
**decline** if the sponsor retained coercive rights.

### 3. Paid work competing with an existing commitment

A practitioner considered paid work that would displace a promised community
release. The framework made the fee, capacity, existing relationship, and
promise visible and supported **revise**, **wait**, a consented handoff, or
**decline**. Commercial value did not erase community obligations or
operational sustainability.

### 4. Assisted external action and batch approval

An AI assistant prepared follow-up messages for workshop attendees, including
one recipient who withdrew consent and drafts that added an unrequested paid
offer. The framework required action-specific human review, **do not contact**
for the withdrawn recipient, revision of unsupported content, and **wait** when
the accountable person could not review each exact action. An approve-all click
did not qualify as accountable judgment.

Across the exercises, contribution, relationship, learning, relevant reach,
sustainability, and commercial outcomes remained separate. Decisions included
proceed, contribute first, research further, revise, wait, decline, no action
needed, and do not contact without turning those labels into a score or
mandatory workflow.

## Limitations

The cases are fictional prose applications, not legal, financial,
safeguarding, accessibility, field-effectiveness, or community-acceptance
reviews. They test whether the canonical business method supports responsible
judgment; they do not certify an implementation or prove that a practitioner
will follow the guidance under pressure.
