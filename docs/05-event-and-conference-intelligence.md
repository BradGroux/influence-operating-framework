# Event and conference intelligence

Event intelligence helps a practitioner contribute, learn, prepare, and follow through. It is not a plan to maximize contacts.

## Event record

Capture the event's public name, organizer, format, location or online mode, start and end dates, recurrence, accessibility information, public sources, proposal deadlines, relevant ecosystems, confidence, privacy, and review date. Verify current details before travel, submission, or outreach.

## Fit questions

- Does the event serve the practitioner's mission and communities?
- What has the organizer or community publicly asked for?
- What can the practitioner contribute before, during, or after attendance?
- Are the format, accessibility, timing, travel, cost, energy, and preparation demands workable?
- Is the goal learning, service, relationship continuity, an artifact, visibility, or a legitimate downstream commercial outcome?
- What would make not attending the better decision?

## Event brief

A brief includes verified logistics, purpose, ecosystem context, contributions, existing relationships, public participation opportunities, accessibility and safety considerations, commitments, open questions, and stop conditions. It must distinguish people the practitioner already knows from people merely listed publicly.

## Conference playbook

Before:

- verify current details and proposal rules;
- choose one to three contribution or learning intentions;
- prepare reusable, accessible artifacts;
- ask consent before arranging introductions;
- draft outreach only when there is a real contextual reason.

During:

- participate in the community's norms;
- prioritize presence and usefulness over collecting contacts;
- record only necessary notes with appropriate privacy;
- honor accessibility, photography, recording, and communication preferences;
- avoid live automation that posts or messages without review.

After:

- fulfill promises before seeking new opportunities;
- share useful artifacts where welcomed;
- choose follow-up, wait, no action, or do not contact individually;
- reflect on expected versus actual community and practitioner value.
