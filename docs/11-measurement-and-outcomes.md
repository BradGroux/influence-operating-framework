# Measurement and outcomes

Measurement supports reflection and resource choices. It does not define human worth, relationship quality, or objective influence.

See the [measurement model](../visuals/exports/svg/measurement-model.svg) and [accessible description](../visuals/descriptions/measurement-model.md).

## Five outcome categories

### Contribution

Useful artifacts, communities helped, introductions completed with consent, people mentored, open-source work, reusable talks or workshops, educational resources, accessibility improvements, and commitments fulfilled.

### Relationships

Meaningful interactions, continuity, mutual value described by evidence, follow-through, introductions given and received with consent, trusted collaborations, repaired commitments, and appropriate no-action choices.

### Learning

Reflections completed, assumptions corrected, failed experiments documented, improvements adopted, reusable lessons, contradictory evidence preserved, and practices stopped when they caused burden or failed to help.

### Reach and influence

Relevant audience change, invitations, repeat invitations, referrals, media participation, speaking opportunities, workshop demand, community recognition, and reuse of practitioner-created artifacts.

### Commercial outcomes

Paid speaking, workshops, consulting, partnerships, and mission-aligned organizational opportunities. These are legitimate outcomes but remain downstream and must not erase community value, consent, capacity, or ethical risk.

## Measurement rules

- Define the question before choosing a measure.
- Keep counts, rates, qualitative observations, and narratives distinguishable.
- State data source, date range, missing data, confidence, and known bias.
- Do not infer causation from sequence or correlation.
- Do not combine categories into a universal influence score.
- Compare against the practitioner's declared mission and capacity, not another person's status.
- Record negative and null outcomes, including work intentionally not pursued.
- Review whether the measure creates gaming, surveillance, pressure, or unnecessary data collection.

## Monthly and annual review

Use measures to ask: What value was created? Which commitments were honored? Which relationships need care or space? What was learned? Which audience was actually served? Which commercial outcomes remained aligned? What should stop? What evidence is missing or stale?
