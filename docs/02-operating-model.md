# Operating model

The lifecycle is a reusable practice loop, not a funnel:

> Discover → Research → Verify → Map → Prioritize → Contribute → Engage → Follow up → Reflect → Improve → Repeat

A stage may pause, repeat, skip, or end in no action. A person being researched never becomes obligated to receive contact. See the [lifecycle visual](../visuals/exports/svg/influence-lifecycle.svg) and [description](../visuals/descriptions/influence-lifecycle.md).

## Discover

- **Purpose:** Notice ecosystems, questions, communities, and possible ways to help that align with the practitioner's mission.
- **Inputs:** Profile, goals, public signals, referrals, community calendars, prior reflections.
- **Outputs:** Research questions and candidate records, not a contact list.
- **Evidence:** Capture discovery source and date; label unsupported possibilities as hypotheses.
- **Human decisions:** Decide relevance, research proportionality, and whether to stop.
- **Automation:** Monitor approved public sources and deduplicate candidate records.
- **Prohibited:** Private scraping, purchased lists, covert monitoring, or engagement based only on status.
- **Quality:** Each candidate has a mission connection and a reason to learn, contribute, or explicitly take no action.
- **Reflection:** What useful signal was found, and what bias shaped attention?

## Research

- **Purpose:** Gather the minimum public or authorized context needed to understand the ecosystem and avoid careless action.
- **Inputs:** Research question, candidate records, source policy, privacy boundary.
- **Outputs:** Evidence records and claims labeled fact, inference, or hypothesis.
- **Evidence:** Prefer primary sources; preserve URLs, dates, excerpts or summaries, confidence, contradiction, and staleness.
- **Human decisions:** Approve sensitive-source use and decide when evidence is sufficient.
- **Automation:** Retrieve approved public sources, normalize citations, and identify conflicts.
- **Prohibited:** Fabrication, unnecessary personal data, role assumptions, or inference presented as fact.
- **Quality:** Claims trace to evidence and current roles are verified before use.
- **Reflection:** Which missing or conflicting fact matters enough to investigate further?

## Verify

- **Purpose:** Test whether material claims are current, supported, and safe to use.
- **Inputs:** Claims, evidence, source hierarchy, freshness rules.
- **Outputs:** Verified, contradicted, stale, uncertain, or rejected claims.
- **Evidence:** Corroborate consequential claims and record why sources agree or conflict.
- **Human decisions:** Resolve whether uncertainty permits planning, requires more research, or stops action.
- **Automation:** Check dates, broken sources, repeated claims, and contradictory values.
- **Prohibited:** Silently choosing the convenient source or upgrading confidence without evidence.
- **Quality:** Verification status and reviewer are explicit; unknown remains a valid result.
- **Reflection:** What assumption failed, and should the research method change?

## Map

- **Purpose:** Connect verified records into an ecosystem view that explains relationships and context.
- **Inputs:** Verified people, organizations, events, communities, channels, projects, relationships, and evidence.
- **Outputs:** Linked records and bounded map views.
- **Evidence:** Every asserted edge has supporting evidence or is marked hypothesis.
- **Human decisions:** Choose map scope, privacy level, and which edges are useful rather than invasive.
- **Automation:** Resolve stable IDs, identify duplicates, and render approved graph views.
- **Prohibited:** Inferring relationship strength from follows, adjacency, employer, or co-attendance alone.
- **Quality:** Nodes and edges are typed, explainable, scoped, and privacy classified.
- **Reflection:** Which missing connection changes understanding, and which detail is unnecessary?

## Prioritize

- **Purpose:** Allocate finite attention to the most mission-aligned learning or contribution possibilities.
- **Inputs:** Profile, map, capacity, opportunities, risks, evidence, existing commitments.
- **Outputs:** Explainable priority, defer, wait, no action, or do-not-contact decisions.
- **Evidence:** Ratings cite evidence and include narrative reasoning and uncertainty.
- **Human decisions:** Set trade-offs, override scores, and protect capacity and boundaries.
- **Automation:** Compare declared dimensions and surface missing evidence; never make the final decision.
- **Prohibited:** Ranking human worth, using follower count alone, or converting research into outreach queues automatically.
- **Quality:** Community value and ethical risk are considered beside practitioner benefit.
- **Reflection:** What did the prioritization method reward unintentionally?

## Contribute

- **Purpose:** Create useful value aligned with actual community needs before or without an ask.
- **Inputs:** Verified need, practitioner capacity, community norms, opportunity, evidence.
- **Outputs:** Contribution plan, artifact, introduction, service, or explicit no-action choice.
- **Evidence:** Record the need, source, intended beneficiary, consent dependencies, and completion evidence.
- **Human decisions:** Commit resources, obtain necessary consent, and judge whether contribution is appropriate.
- **Automation:** Suggest reusable artifacts, check accessibility, and track approved commitments.
- **Prohibited:** Performative help, conditional reciprocity, unsolicited burden, or fabricated endorsement.
- **Quality:** Value is concrete, proportionate, accessible, and not contingent on receiving attention.
- **Reflection:** Who benefited, who bore cost, and what should change?

## Engage

- **Purpose:** Participate or communicate respectfully when evidence, contribution, timing, consent, and human judgment support it.
- **Inputs:** Context, contribution, draft, communication preferences, do-not-contact state.
- **Outputs:** Human disposition and, only after separate authorization, an external-system handoff.
- **Evidence:** Draft references truthful context and the reviewer can inspect supporting evidence.
- **Human decisions:** Approve, revise, wait, or do not contact; approval does not send.
- **Automation:** Draft, check tone and evidence, and log a disposition.
- **Prohibited:** Autonomous sending, false familiarity, pressure, mass personalization, urgency, flattery, or bypassing a boundary.
- **Quality:** The message is concise, contextual, respectful, easy to decline, and contains no unsupported claim.
- **Reflection:** Was engagement necessary, welcomed, and consistent with the contribution-first principle?

## Follow up

- **Purpose:** Honor commitments and maintain relevant continuity without manufacturing activity.
- **Inputs:** Actual interaction, promises, communication preferences, timing, outcome.
- **Outputs:** Fulfilled commitment, recommendation to follow up, wait, no action, or do not contact.
- **Evidence:** Record only what occurred and what was explicitly promised.
- **Human decisions:** Decide timing, channel, relevance, and whether any follow-up should occur.
- **Automation:** Remind about approved commitments and draft a contextual note.
- **Prohibited:** Cadence spam, invented urgency, repeated contact after silence, or ignoring do not contact.
- **Quality:** Follow-up has a truthful reason and respects recipient preference and burden.
- **Reflection:** Did continuity create mutual value, or merely more activity?

## Reflect

- **Purpose:** Turn outcomes, rejections, surprises, failures, and successes into evidence-backed learning.
- **Inputs:** Plan, interaction, contribution, outcome evidence, expectations.
- **Outputs:** Reflection, challenged assumptions, lessons, artifact ideas, and proposed changes.
- **Evidence:** Separate observed outcome from interpretation and hindsight.
- **Human decisions:** Choose which lesson is credible, private, shareable, or ready for governance review.
- **Automation:** Assemble source records and suggest patterns while preserving dissent.
- **Prohibited:** Success-only reporting, blame, revisionist history, or automatic policy change.
- **Quality:** Expected versus actual is explicit and failed experiments receive equal attention.
- **Reflection:** What will repeat, stop, change, or be tested next?

## Improve

- **Purpose:** Convert credible lessons into controlled changes to profiles, practices, templates, or proposed framework guidance.
- **Inputs:** Reflections, outcomes, incidents, community feedback, changed constraints.
- **Outputs:** Accepted small change, experiment, proposal, ADR candidate, or no change.
- **Evidence:** Link each change to the lesson and affected artifacts.
- **Human decisions:** Approve experiments, migrations, governance path, and rollback.
- **Automation:** Identify affected records, draft change notes, and run validation.
- **Prohibited:** Silent principle changes, metric gaming, or generalizing from one outcome without review.
- **Quality:** The change is proportionate, reversible where possible, validated, and documented.
- **Reflection:** Did the change improve real practice without weakening safeguards?

## Repeat

- **Purpose:** Begin another bounded cycle with updated context rather than accumulating an endless queue.
- **Inputs:** Updated profile, map, capacity, open commitments, lessons, and stopped work.
- **Outputs:** A fresh discovery question or an intentional pause.
- **Evidence:** Carry forward authoritative state and mark obsolete assumptions.
- **Human decisions:** Decide whether, where, and when another cycle is worthwhile.
- **Automation:** Surface changed evidence and unresolved commitments.
- **Prohibited:** Automatic re-enrollment, perpetual outreach, or treating inactivity as failure.
- **Quality:** The next cycle has a clear mission reason, bounded scope, and stop condition.
- **Reflection:** Is another cycle the best use of community and practitioner attention?

## Completion and recovery

A cycle is complete when its intended contribution, decision, or learning outcome is verified and commitments are accounted for. It may stop safely because evidence is insufficient, capacity changed, consent is absent, harm risk increased, or no action is appropriate. Recovery means preserving the last authoritative state, explaining the stop, notifying the accountable human when needed, and resuming only after the blocking condition changes.
