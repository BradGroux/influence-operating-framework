# Contribution strategy

Contribution is useful value offered without making attention, access, endorsement, or reciprocity a condition.

## Find a real need

Begin with evidence from maintainers, organizers, participants, issue trackers, published agendas, accessibility notes, community discussions, or direct requests. Avoid inventing a need because it fits an existing talk or service.

## Choose a proportionate contribution

Evaluate:

- beneficiary and publicly expressed need;
- mission and skill fit;
- consent and community norms;
- time, money, energy, accessibility, and maintenance cost;
- who else is already doing the work;
- whether the contribution creates more leaders or centralizes attention;
- reuse, handoff, and long-term stewardship;
- potential harm, displacement, self-promotion, or unwanted burden.

Possible contributions include documentation, issue triage, code, mentoring with consent, an introduction with bilateral consent, accessible summaries, teaching material, workshop facilitation, volunteer support, feedback, sponsorship without control, or amplifying requested work.

## Contribution plan

A plan states the need and evidence, intended value, beneficiaries, collaborators, consent dependencies, scope, owner, due window, completion evidence, accessibility check, maintenance or handoff, risks, and what is explicitly not expected in return.

## Before an ask

Contribution does not purchase a later ask. If an ask becomes appropriate, evaluate it independently against context, timing, recipient burden, communication preference, and human judgment. Often the correct next action remains sharing a public artifact, waiting, or taking no action.

## Quality review

- The need is evidenced, not assumed.
- The community can decline or redirect the offer.
- The contribution is accessible and maintainable enough for its context.
- Credit and ownership are clear.
- Beneficiary value is not measured only by practitioner visibility.
- Completion includes evidence and a reflection, including failure or unintended cost.
