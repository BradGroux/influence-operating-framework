# Influence Operating Framework charter

**Status:** Accepted initial baseline
**Owner:** Brad Groux
**Effective date:** 2026-08-01

## Mission

Help practitioners build meaningful influence by contributing useful value, sustaining respectful relationships, learning from evidence, and improving repeatable practices.

## Vision

People and mission-led groups can participate in communities with more clarity and care because purpose, evidence, boundaries, decisions, contributions, engagement, and learning are explicit and shared.

## Locked principles

1. **Leave every community better than you found it.**
2. **Create more leaders, not more followers.**
3. **Value first.**
4. **Relationships over transactions.**
5. **Own the framework, not the tool.**
6. **Growth and monetization are outcomes, not the central goal.**
7. **Public by default, private where ethics, consent, or safety require it.**
8. **Human judgment governs external engagement.**
9. **Every success and failure should improve the system.**
10. **The framework is living, reflective, and anti-fragile.**
11. **The journey belongs to the practitioner; the framework provides the map.**
12. **No generic spam, engagement farming, or deceptive automation.**

## What the framework establishes

- a lifecycle for discovering, verifying, mapping, contributing, engaging, and learning without treating people as a funnel;
- a graph-friendly vocabulary and portable record layer for ecosystems, evidence, relationships, opportunities, and outcomes;
- a research method that preserves sources, uncertainty, contradiction, staleness, and privacy;
- a human disposition boundary for every external engagement;
- a contribution-first practice for speaking, teaching, media, community, technical, philanthropic, educational, spiritual, and commercial missions;
- bounded agent contracts that can assist without sending or fabricating;
- a visual grammar and validation gates that make the framework inspectable.

## Scope

The framework covers ecosystem and event intelligence, relationship continuity, contribution planning, opportunity evaluation, ethical outreach drafting, follow-up, reflection, outcome measurement, reusable profiles, private-overlay boundaries, and safe automation specifications.

It serves individual practitioners and mission-led groups. Brad Groux's speaking and influence journey is the first profile, not the definition of the framework.

## Non-goals

The framework does not:

- treat people as leads, prospects, targets, or conversion probabilities;
- rank worth by audience size, role, status, revenue, or access;
- send messages or authorize autonomous external actions;
- scrape private data, bypass controls, buy contact lists, or enable mass outreach;
- fabricate facts, roles, event history, relationships, familiarity, or evidence;
- guarantee invitations, influence, speaking engagements, recognition, or revenue;
- prescribe a vendor, database, CRM, automation platform, model, or hosted service;
- replace consent, professional judgment, law, policy, safety, accessibility, or community norms.

## Accountability

The practitioner remains accountable for purpose, evidence interpretation, prioritization, contribution, external engagement, privacy, and lessons adopted. Agents may assist only within approved contracts. A human can always stop work, request more evidence, take no action, wait, or mark do not contact.

## Authority and change

[Locked decisions](../decisions/locked-decisions.md) preserve this charter. Canonical guidance may evolve through [governance](../GOVERNANCE.md), but lower-authority schemas, examples, profiles, tools, or metrics cannot silently change its meaning.
