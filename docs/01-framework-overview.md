# Framework overview

Influence is earned capacity to help ideas, people, and communities move. The framework treats it as a living practice, not a number someone owns.

## Four connected layers

1. **Purpose and profile** — mission, goals, boundaries, strengths, capacity, ecosystems, channels, and review cadence.
2. **Shared evidence graph** — people, organizations, events, relationships, interactions, opportunities, contributions, outcomes, and supporting evidence linked by stable IDs.
3. **Operating practices** — discover, research, verify, map, prioritize, contribute, engage, follow up, reflect, improve, and repeat.
4. **Governance and assurance** — authority, privacy, human decisions, prohibited actions, validation, change records, and lessons.

See the [framework overview visual](../visuals/exports/svg/framework-overview.svg) and its [accessible description](../visuals/descriptions/framework-overview.md).

## Contribution before ask

The default direction is outward: understand a community, learn what it values, verify the context, and contribute something useful. An ask is not required. Contribution does not purchase attention, access, or reciprocity.

A contribution can be an introduction, documentation improvement, open-source change, educational artifact, volunteer effort, thoughtful feedback, accessible resource, workshop, talk, or amplification requested by the community.

## Relationship continuity

The framework records what actually happened, what was promised, what context is relevant, and which boundaries apply. It does not infer closeness from frequency, audience size, follows, likes, or a shared event. “No action needed,” “wait,” and “do not contact” are first-class outcomes.

## Evidence before confidence

Every material research claim is a fact, inference, or hypothesis. Sources, access dates, confidence, contradictions, and staleness remain visible. Missing information stays missing until verified; an agent must never fill a gap with plausible detail.

## Human control

Automation may collect public evidence, find conflicts, draft briefs, propose contributions, prepare outreach drafts, or synthesize reflections. It may not send external communication. A human disposition is required, and approval only makes a separately controlled handoff possible.

## Outcomes without a single scoreboard

The framework observes contribution, relationship continuity, learning, relevant reach, and commercial outcomes separately. Counts require narrative context. The aim is compounding usefulness and trust, not maximizing activity.
