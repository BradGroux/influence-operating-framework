# Reflection and anti-fragile learning

Reflection makes success, rejection, failure, surprise, and ordinary completion useful to future practice.

> Learn → Document → Evolve → Repeat

See the [learning loop](../visuals/exports/svg/reflection-learning-loop.svg) and [description](../visuals/descriptions/reflection-learning-loop.md).

## When to reflect

Reflect after talks, workshops, conferences, proposal acceptance or rejection, outreach, introductions, podcast or media participation, community contributions, experiments, unexpected opportunities, missed commitments, privacy or safety concerns, and meaningful no-action decisions.

## Reflection record

Capture:

- what happened and the relevant record IDs;
- expected versus actual outcome;
- observed evidence versus interpretation;
- what worked, failed, or surprised;
- who benefited and who carried cost;
- which assumption was strengthened or challenged;
- what should repeat, stop, change, or be tested;
- what useful artifact or handoff should be created;
- whether a profile, workflow, template, schema, or locked decision needs review;
- privacy classification and who may read the record.

## Anti-fragile does not mean harm-seeking

The framework does not create failures to prove resilience or romanticize preventable harm. Anti-fragile learning means that real outcomes—especially uncomfortable ones—can improve the practice when captured honestly, reviewed proportionately, and translated into controlled changes.

## From lesson to change

1. Link the lesson to evidence and affected artifacts.
2. State whether it is local to one profile or potentially general.
3. Look for contradictory cases and affected-community perspectives.
4. Choose a reversible experiment when confidence is limited.
5. Use governance for canonical or locked changes.
6. Validate, record the disposition, and preserve dissent.

An agent may synthesize patterns and draft a proposal. It may not amend the framework, erase an exception, or declare a lesson universal.
