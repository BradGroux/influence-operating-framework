# Example: relationship continuity

- **Provenance:** Fictional
- **Review status:** Illustrative; not community-validated

## Scenario

During the fictional Northbank Open Knowledge Workshop, facilitator Jules
Okafor introduces participant Lina Moreno to Mara Ellis. Lina asks Mara whether
she can share a captioning checklist used in an earlier workshop. Mara says she
will send the public link the following week.

The workshop ends without any conversation about future work. Lina follows
Mara's public writing account two days later.

## Six-concern review

### Purpose

The immediate purpose is to fulfill a specific promise. It is not to convert a
new introduction into a recurring relationship or opportunity.

### Context

Mara records only what is necessary for continuity: Jules made the
introduction, Lina requested a public resource, and Mara promised to provide the
link. The social follow is visible but does not prove familiarity, endorsement,
or interest in further contact.

### Contribution

Mara checks that the checklist is still current and sends the promised public
link with one sentence of context. She does not add a pitch, meeting request, or
newsletter subscription.

### Relationship

The shared history is limited but truthful. A direct introduction and fulfilled
promise now exist. No commitment exists beyond the resource, and Mara does not
invent a next step.

### Judgment

After sending the link, Mara chooses **no action needed**. The promise is
complete. She will respond if Lina asks a question, but she does not schedule a
generic check-in merely to keep the relationship active.

### Learning

Lina later uses the checklist in a volunteer training session and publicly
credits the source. Mara learns this only from Lina's public post. She notes the
evidence that the resource was useful but does not treat the credit as consent
for direct promotion.

The practical lesson is that reliable follow-through can be the whole value of
an interaction. Continuity does not require constant activity.

## What the example avoids

- Treating the introduction as a lead.
- Treating the social follow as proof of relationship strength.
- Adding an unrequested ask to a promised resource.
- Creating an automated follow-up cadence.
- Claiming endorsement from public credit.
