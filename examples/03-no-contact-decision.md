# Example: no-contact decision

- **Provenance:** Fictional
- **Review status:** Illustrative; not community-validated

## Scenario

The fictional Lakeshore Civic Media Gathering publishes an open call for
workshop proposals. Its organizer page also says that unsolicited speaker
pitches sent to individual organizers will not be reviewed and asks applicants
to use the public submission process.

Mara Ellis finds the gathering relevant to her accessibility education work.
She also finds an organizer's public professional profile and could technically
send a direct message.

## Six-concern review

### Purpose

Mara wants to share a tested workshop that could help small civic organizations
publish accessible media. The purpose can be served through the open call; it
does not require personal access to an organizer.

### Context

The current event site provides the theme, audience, deadline, review criteria,
accessibility expectations, and official submission channel. The organizer's
published boundary is explicit. No additional personal research is necessary.

### Contribution

Mara prepares a proposal with concrete participant outcomes, an accessible
resource participants may reuse, honest prerequisites, and a clear statement
of what the workshop cannot cover.

### Relationship

Mara has no prior interaction with the organizer. A discoverable profile does
not create one. The published no-pitch boundary applies regardless of whether a
direct message might increase visibility.

### Judgment

Mara makes two decisions:

1. **Do not contact** the organizer about the proposal.
2. **Proceed** through the public submission process.

The first decision does not prevent legitimate participation. It keeps the
participation within the community's stated process.

### Learning

The proposal is declined with a short note that the agenda already contains a
similar workshop. Mara records the actual reason and does not reinterpret the
decision as personal rejection.

She compares the accepted agenda with her proposal and identifies a narrower
resource gap: accessible captions for short civic videos. She publishes a
standalone guide without using the event or organizers as a promotional hook.

## Result

Respecting a contact boundary did not block contribution or learning. The
framework supported a public submission, an accountable no-contact decision,
and a useful artifact after rejection—without evasion, pressure, or a private
outreach sequence.
