# Example: conference contribution

- **Provenance:** Fictional
- **Review status:** Illustrative; not community-validated

## Scenario

Mara Ellis teaches practical accessibility techniques to regional software
teams. She is considering attending the fictional Riverbend Community
Technology Forum. She initially thinks about proposing a talk, but the forum's
public planning notes say that first-time contributors and small nonprofits are
struggling to make workshop materials accessible.

Mara has one day available before the proposal deadline. She has never met the
organizers and has no private information about participants.

## Six-concern review

### Purpose

Mara's purpose is to help community educators make their materials usable by
more participants. A speaking slot could support that purpose, but visibility
alone would not justify the work.

The intended beneficiaries are workshop facilitators and attendees who need
accessible documents. Mara will stop if the forum already has a maintained
resource or if an organizer says another contribution would be more useful.

### Context

Mara reviews the forum's current public site, published accessibility guidance,
and open planning notes. She distinguishes three things:

- **Observed fact:** The proposal deadline and contribution process appear on
  the current event site.
- **Observed fact:** Planning notes request help with accessible workshop
  materials.
- **Open question:** Whether organizers want a new guide, feedback on existing
  material, or volunteer review time.

She does not build a list of speakers, search for private contact information,
or assume that a public organizer profile creates familiarity.

### Contribution

Mara drafts a two-page starter checklist using the forum's published guidance.
She includes headings, image descriptions, color contrast, keyboard access,
captions, and accessible document exports. The draft is openly licensed and
designed so the forum can edit or reject it.

She offers the checklist through the forum's public contribution channel and
makes clear that no speaking opportunity or attribution is expected.

### Relationship

There is no existing personal relationship. Mara describes herself as a
prospective contributor, not a friend of the organizers or a member of their
team. She makes one bounded offer through the channel the forum published.

### Judgment

Mara chooses **contribute first**. She does not submit the original talk idea
yet because the evidenced need is more immediate and her available day is
enough to create a useful draft.

Her stop conditions are clear: no unsolicited direct messages, no repeated
follow-up if the contribution receives no response, and no public claim that
the forum uses or endorses the checklist unless organizers say so.

### Learning

The maintainers accept several checklist items but explain that they already
have a longer guide. They ask Mara to improve two examples instead of publishing
a competing document. After the event, an open attendee-feedback thread and the
organizer's public lessons specifically request a short guide to captioning
demonstration videos.

Mara records that her assumption about the missing artifact was wrong, while
the underlying need was real. She contributes the two examples, abandons the
new checklist, and treats the captioning request as a separately evidenced
future contribution rather than inferring a gap from the agenda. The lesson is
to look for maintained community resources and direct community signals before
creating a replacement.

## Result

The useful outcome is a smaller contribution than Mara first imagined. There is
no speaking slot, outreach sequence, or claim of a new relationship. The forum
receives improvements it requested, Mara learns its contribution norms, and no
maintenance burden is transferred without agreement.
