# Charter

- **Status:** Accepted 1.0.0-rc.4 release candidate
- **Founding steward:** Brad Groux
- **Effective date:** 2026-08-01

## Definition

The Influence Operating Framework is an open business operating framework and
method for building meaningful influence through useful contribution, trusted
relationships, accountable judgment, and continuous learning.

Influence is the earned capacity to help ideas, people, and communities move.
It is practiced with others, not accumulated over them.

## Mission

Help practitioners participate in communities with purpose, understand context
without manufacturing certainty, contribute useful value, sustain respectful
relationships, make sound decisions, and learn from outcomes.

## Who it serves

The framework serves individuals and mission-led groups. It can be applied to
community participation, education, open source, speaking, media, workshops,
philanthropy, public service, professional networks, and commercial work.

The practitioner remains accountable. Colleagues, researchers, software, and
AI may assist, but assistance does not transfer responsibility for judgment or
external action.

## Commitments

1. **Leave every community better than you found it.** Participation should
   create or preserve value for the community, not merely extract attention.
2. **Create more leaders, not more followers.** Share capability, credit, and
   opportunity instead of making the practitioner indispensable.
3. **Value first.** Begin with a real need and a useful contribution, not an
   entitlement to access or response.
4. **Relationships over transactions.** Treat continuity, trust, consent, and
   commitments as more important than activity counts.
5. **Own the framework, not the tool.** Practice should survive changes in
   software, platforms, vendors, and channels.
6. **Growth and monetization are outcomes, not the center.** They may be valid,
   but they do not define human worth or community value.
7. **Public by default, private where ethics, consent, or safety require it.**
   Transparency does not justify exposing personal or sensitive context.
8. **Human judgment governs external engagement.** No tool or score decides
   that a person should be contacted.
9. **Every success and failure should improve the practice.** Rejection,
   surprise, restraint, and ordinary completion are legitimate evidence.
10. **The framework is living and reflective.** It evolves through observed
    practice, challenge, and accountable change.
11. **The journey belongs to the practitioner; the framework provides the
    map.** It supports judgment without replacing agency.
12. **No spam, engagement farming, or deceptive automation.** False
    familiarity, manufactured urgency, covert targeting, and mass
    personalization violate the framework.

## What the framework establishes

The framework establishes:

- six concerns that responsible influence practice must make clear;
- a practical method for orienting, understanding, choosing, contributing,
  engaging, sustaining, and learning;
- standards for evidence, privacy, consent, human judgment, and accountable
  external action;
- an outcome model that separates contribution, relationship, learning, reach,
  and commercial effects; and
- a maintenance practice for turning credible lessons into proportionate
  improvements.

## What it does not establish

The framework does not:

- treat people as leads, prospects, targets, assets, or conversion
  probabilities;
- rank human value by role, audience, revenue, access, or relationship status;
- guarantee invitations, recognition, influence, speaking engagements, or
  revenue;
- replace law, policy, professional judgment, safeguarding, accessibility, or
  community norms;
- authorize scraping, private-data collection, autonomous communication, or
  mass outreach;
- prescribe a CRM, database, schema, API, agent protocol, record format,
  automation architecture, model, vendor, or hosted service; or
- require a universal sequence, score, maturity model, or certification.

Technical implementations may support the framework. They remain local
implementation choices and do not become framework requirements.

## Accountability

The practitioner is accountable for:

- declaring purpose and intended beneficiaries;
- deciding what evidence is sufficient and what remains uncertain;
- limiting research and retained context to what is proportionate;
- ensuring that contribution is useful rather than performative;
- honoring consent, communication preferences, promises, and stop conditions;
- approving every external action through the actual system that performs it;
  and
- examining outcomes honestly and adopting lessons carefully.

A practitioner may always stop, narrow the activity, wait, decline, take no
action, or decide not to contact someone.

## Stewardship and change

The founding steward maintains the framework through
[governance](../GOVERNANCE.md). Examples, tools, training, and implementations
may interpret the framework for a context, but they cannot silently change its
meaning.
